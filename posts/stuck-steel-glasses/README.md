## Story
**Arc in one line:** no glue, yet they won't come apart → so who's holding them? → the taper wedges the
inner glass in → a film of water seals the rim → pulling grows the gap but no air can get in → pressure
drops and the outside air presses them together → twist: the air you can't even see is holding them → loop.

**Why it works:** the hook is something every Indian home has fought with. The question "कौन?" makes it a
small case to solve, and each line answers the one before it. The twist answers "कौन?" with something
invisible, puts it on the viewer ("जिसे आप देख भी नहीं सकते"), and calls back to the hook's "कोई glue
नहीं": the air *is* the glue. On replay, "…वही हवा असली glue है" runs straight into "इन दो glass के बीच
कोई glue नहीं…", so the loop reads as one thought.

**Language:** Hinglish, the way people actually talk. A word is in English **only if people normally say
it in English** in daily speech (glass, glue, tight, layer, gap, pressure). Everything else stays in plain
Hindi (पकड़, चौड़ा, पतला, किनारे, खींचते, बंद, कम), never swapped for English just to sound mixed. "glass"
stays singular after "दो" (दो glass), as people say it, so it isn't heard as "glasses" (spectacles).

## Mechanism check
Tapered tumblers wedge into each other, so the inner glass seats snugly against the outer wall. A film of
water (from washing) seals the hairline gap at the rim. When you pull, the sealed space between the two
bottoms gets bigger, but no air can get in, so its pressure drops below the outside air pressure. The
outside air then presses the two glasses together. Stacking them warm makes it worse: the trapped air cools,
and its pressure drops even before you pull.

**How sure we are:** the partial-vacuum (sealed-gap) explanation is the most commonly cited one, but the
sources are blogs and forums, not lab measurements. Friction from the wedge and water adhesion also
contribute. The VO says what's true in all cases: the seal keeps air out and the outside air presses in.
It doesn't claim air is the *only* force.

**The fix (for the pinned comment and description):** pour cold water into the inner glass and stand the
outer glass in hot water. Steel expands with heat (more than glass or ceramic), so the outer glass opens up
slightly while the inner one shrinks. The warm water also warms the trapped air, so its pressure rises. Then
twist gently, don't yank.

Sources: [Chad Orzel — The physics of stuck bowls](https://chadorzel.substack.com/p/the-physics-of-stuck-bowls)
(a physicist names three candidates: interlocking, water adhesion, partial vacuum from warm air cooling) ·
[ScienceForums — why do wet plates stick together](https://scienceforums.net/topic/98473-why-do-wet-plates-stick-together)
(water seals the gap, so separating them makes a vacuum) ·
[Hunker — How to unstick plastic glasses](https://www.hunker.com/13420130/how-to-unstick-plastic-glasses/)
(warm air cools, pressure drops, outside air presses). Checked 17 Nov 2026.
