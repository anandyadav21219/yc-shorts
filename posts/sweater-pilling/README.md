**Arc in one line:** the pills don't come from outside, they *are* your sweater → every yarn is short fibres with loose ends → rubbing pulls the ends out → they tangle into a ball → twist: it never falls off because its fibres won't break → so the pill is a sign of strength, not weakness → loop.

**Why it works:** the hook contradicts what viewers assume (fluff or dirt from outside) and tells them the pills
*are* their sweater. The on-screen question already asks "why", so the script skips a mystery line and goes
straight to the culprit. Every line is 12 words or fewer, so nothing is rushed. The twist answers the question
everyone with an old sweater has ("why won't these come off?") and flips the belief that a pill means weak
fabric: the pills that stay are held by fibres too strong to break. The last line ends on "ये गोली…" and the
replay opens on "ये गोलियाँ…".

## Mechanism check
Rubbing at high-wear spots (cuffs, collar, under the arms) pulls the ends of short fibres out of the yarn
(fuzz) → the loose ends tangle and roll into a ball (pill) → the pill stays tied on by fibres that haven't
broken. Weak fibres such as wool break, so wool's pills fall away over time. Strong synthetic fibres (polyester,
nylon, acrylic) hold their pills on. In a blend, the weaker fibre wears and breaks while the stronger one
anchors the pill. The industry names four stages: fuzz formation, entanglement, growth, wear-off. Pills are the
fabric's own fibres. Knits pill more than wovens. Longer fibres and tighter knits pill less.

Kept out of the VO (one twist only), but used in the description: pure wool sheds its pills on its own.
