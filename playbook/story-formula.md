# Story formula — 25–33 sec Shorts

Our house formula, distilled from our first scripts (cooker whistle, sweater pilling).
The job: stop the scroll in 2 seconds, turn the mechanism into a small mystery, and end on a
true twist that loops straight back into the start.

## The arc — 5–6 beats, one idea per clip

| Beat | ~Time | Job | Example |
|------|-------|-----|---------|
| 1 · Hook | 0–3s | Show the satisfying action **and** say something that contradicts or reframes it | ये गोलियाँ बाहर से नहीं आतीं… आपके स्वेटर से ही बनती हैं। *(while the camera pushes in on them)* |
| 2 · Mystery | 3–7s | Turn the hook into one concrete question — best as a contrast (tight knit yet fuzz, tiny input yet big result) | धागा तो कसकर बुना है… फिर रोएँ आते कहाँ से हैं? |
| 3 · Culprit | 7–12s | A cutaway reveals the hidden part that answers it; give the part a role (guard, gatekeeper) | ऊपर रखा ये छोटा-सा वज़न दरबान की तरह है। |
| 4 · How | 12–19s | The cause → effect chain, one link per clip, short verbs in a row; a sound word on the money shot | फिर रेशे उलझते हैं, लिपटते हैं… और बन जाती है गोली! |
| 5 · Twist | last 6–8s | One true fact that reframes the hook — strongest when it lands on the viewer | पर ये गिरती क्यों नहीं? इसे थामे रेशे टूटने का नाम नहीं लेते। |
| 6 · Loop | final 1–2s | Last frame = clip 1's first frame; the last line should run into the first line on replay | …आपके स्वेटर पर टिकी गोली… मज़बूती की निशानी है। → ये गोलियाँ… |

**Hook types that suit the channel**
- **Contradiction** — the picture says one thing, the voice says the opposite (pills sitting on the sweater / "they don't come from outside").
- **Shared moment** — a sound or sight every Indian home knows ("हर भारतीय किचन में ये आवाज़…").
- **Hidden truth** — "X doesn't do Y, it does Z" ("कुकर सीटी नहीं बजाता — साँस छोड़ता है").

Pair any of them with the on-screen topic question (NICHE.md: upper third, added in CapCut).

## Rules
- **Each clip answers the question the previous one raised.** Repeating the question word
  (किसने? … किसने?) gives the script rhythm and makes it feel like a case being solved.
- **The twist must be true and fact-checked.** It becomes the title (NICHE.md → title frames)
  and the line people quote in the comments.
- **One twist only.** Two twists in 30 seconds and neither lands.
- **No parts lists, numbers or units** unless the number *is* the surprise.
- **Talk to the viewer as आप** — warm and respectful; the twist hits hardest when it's about them.
- **No "subscribe" line at the end** — it breaks the loop. CTAs go in the description and pinned comment.
- **End on clip 1's first frame** (freeze it in CapCut) so the replay is seamless.

## Hindi voiceover

Higgsfield speaks the narration inside each clip, so the script is written **one line per clip**,
not as one long take (see [grok-imagine-higgsfield.md](grok-imagine-higgsfield.md)).

**Per-clip budget:** clips come out at ~6 s minimum. Keep each line to **~12 Hindi words at most**
(about 4–5 s of speech), so it never sounds rushed and the clip can be trimmed around it. A
line that needs more words is two clips.

**Cutting to a shorter target:** drop the mystery clip first when the on-screen hook text
already asks the question, then shorten lines to 12 words or fewer. Never drop the twist.
(Sweater pilling went from 7 clips, 86 words and ~35 s to 6 clips, 66 words and ~27 s this way.)

**Pace (starting estimate):** post 1's Hindi narration ran at **≈ 2.75 words/sec**, natural
pauses included. That was measured on an ElevenLabs take, so re-measure on the first Higgsfield
post and update this line.

| Target length | Total word budget | Clips (≤12 words each) |
|---------------|-------------------|------------------------|
| 25 s | ~69 | 6 |
| 28 s | ~77 | 6–7 |
| 30 s | ~82 | 7 |
| 33 s | ~90 | 7–8 |
| 45 s | ~124 | 10–11 |

Take ~3 words off for each deliberate pause or SFX beat (e.g. "…और खटाक!"). The full script is
still written out once in the README so it reads as one story, but each line is generated with
its own clip.

**Wording**
- Everyday Hindi names for parts (डंडी, तीलियाँ, कुंडी, ढक्कन, वज़न) over textbook terms.
- English loanwords where people actually use them, written in Devanagari (स्प्रिंग, प्रेशर, ऑटोमैटिक, लॉक, टाइम).
- Short clauses; "…" and "—" for pauses.
- Sound words for the money shot: खटाक, झट से, फट से, सीटी.
- Pick one tense and keep it.
